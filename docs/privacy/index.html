Privacy policy

Last updated September 18, 2026

TishOS is my personal experiment in organizing my life's data in Markdown. I develop it under the GitHub name ZachTish, and I am currently the app's only user. It is in personal development and internal TestFlight testing.

This policy documents how the native companion handles my information and how this public website handles visits and correspondence. It is provided for transparency and review. Publishing this page does not open the app to other users or make an unfinished integration available.

The app has no public account-registration service, advertising, or third-party analytics SDK. I do not use it to collect other people's vaults or sell personal data. Some optional features use Apple services or my chosen vault-sync provider, so describing the whole system as strictly on-device would be inaccurate.

1. Vault and device data

I select and authorize an Obsidian vault on each device. TishOS reads supported note text, titles, properties, paths, dates, links, and selected plugin configuration to prepare views and resolve supported actions. It stores local preferences, file-access authorization, connection state, and derived caches. Pairing credentials are kept separately in device-local credential storage.

The Markdown files remain in my vault. The companion does not upload a complete vault to a separate developer-operated service. Supported non-sensitive preferences can be backed up in the authorized vault; those files follow the vault's normal synchronization behavior.

The companion normally reads and presents information. Specific, explicitly authorized features can make limited changes, such as supported task capture, a daily activity-summary output, or completion of an existing AI request. Other actions open Obsidian so the responsible plugin can perform the operation. Obsidian, TPS plugins, and any external providers configured in them are separate from the native companion.

2. Widgets, notifications, Live Activities, and search

The app shares limited local display data with its widget extension. Depending on the feature, that data can include note titles and excerpts, calendar details, statistics, or daily macro summaries. Some note-preview data is prepared before a particular widget is placed. Widgets read the local cache and do not independently fetch vault information over the network.

Notifications use the verified Controller reminder schedule. Live Activities can show a selected active item or timer. Optional Siri and Spotlight integration can index supported metadata and, with a separate setting, readable previews. These features can expose information on Apple system surfaces, including the Lock Screen.

I control the feature settings and Apple permissions on each device. Screen visibility and redaction also depend on the operating system. A sensitive value in an ordinary title or preview can still be visible to someone looking at an unlocked device or an enabled notification. Apple's services are governed by its privacy policy and system settings.

3. Synchronization and cloud storage

There are two separate existing paths:

  • Vault-file synchronization. The storage or sync provider I choose for my vault can copy its files between devices. This includes supported exports, portable settings, and AI request files written there. Providers such as iCloud Drive, Obsidian Sync, or Dropbox have their own encryption, backup, sharing, and retention behavior.
  • Optional companion Relay. When enabled, Relay uses my Apple account's private CloudKit database to transfer limited workspace views between authorized devices. These can include selected excerpts, calendar and summary data, configured search content, Live Activity context, and reminder display details. Channel information uses Apple's iCloud key-value storage.

Relay is not a full vault backup. It excludes file-access bookmarks, pairing credentials, unrestricted Markdown, and general command authority. A receiving device must independently authorize its vault and applicable features.

Apple and the selected file provider operate their services. Their policies govern their processing locations and independent retained copies. Private CloudKit storage does not imply a blanket end-to-end-encryption guarantee for every part of this system.

4. Apple Health and experimental Watch features

Apple Health integration is off by default. Nutrition export and activity import are independently selected, with Apple authorization for the categories involved.

  • Nutrition export writes supported food records from my vault to Apple Health. Depending on the build and selections, categories include energy, protein, carbohydrates, total and individual fats, fiber, sugar, sodium, cholesterol, potassium, caffeine, and water.
  • Activity import reads current-day totals for steps, active energy, exercise time, walking/running distance, and flights climbed.

These data are used for my selected nutrition and activity features, not advertising, profiling, or data mining. The private Health synchronization ledger and activity snapshot are stored locally with data protection and excluded from app-managed cloud Relay and device backup. A supported daily activity widget uses a separate, limited local copy of steps and exercise minutes.

Some internal builds include separately confirmed export of daily activity summaries into ordinary vault files. My chosen file provider may synchronize those files, and configured views may display them. This remains an internal-test feature subject to public-distribution review; consent does not create an exception to Apple's restrictions on storing personal health information in iCloud. The private Health ledger and raw sample history are not part of that export.

Experimental Watch features retain manual workout sets, timing, and quick-capture drafts in protected, backup-excluded local files. These can transfer to the paired iPhone through Watch Connectivity and be exported as readable documents after confirmation. A food or cash draft does not automatically create a completed nutrition or finance record.

Optional Watch HealthKit workout processing separately requests permission to save a workout. Sensor metrics are not included in the manual workout journal, its transfer, or its document export. Physical-device and distribution validation for these experimental features remains incomplete.

5. Apple Intelligence requests

Supported builds can process an explicitly requested TPS AI Gateway text job with Apple's on-device model. The companion reads and validates the addressed request in my authorized vault and writes a structured result or failure back to that file. It does not automatically submit the whole vault to a model.

Prompts and results can remain in the vault queue and be copied by its sync provider. Completion does not guarantee immediate deletion of the request file. The current native integration does not have approval for managed Private Cloud Compute access. Other AI providers configured in Obsidian plugins are separate integrations with their own settings and policies.

6. Planned FinanceKit connection

I am seeking FinanceKit approval. This connection is not implemented or approved, and the current companion does not read Apple Wallet financial data.

I would like to authorize the native app to read my supported Apple Card, Savings, and optionally Apple Cash account details, balances, and transactions. The purpose is my own budgeting, spending review, and net-worth tracking in Markdown.

The proposed path is my authorized iPhone, an encrypted financial-data relay through my vault's sync provider, my paired Controller device, and finally account and transaction notes maintained by TPS Finances. This planned transfer is separate from the existing CloudKit display Relay. Resulting Markdown notes would follow the vault's normal storage, backup, and sharing settings; an encrypted transport does not independently encrypt every resulting file.

No payment initiation is planned. I will document the actual permission, storage, retention, and deletion behavior after implementation and before using the connection. This description is a development plan rather than a claim of tested operation.

7. Retention, disconnection, and deletion

My files, device caches, Apple records, and provider backups are separate stores. Disconnecting one feature is not the same as erasing all of them.

Local settings, views, and search indexes
The app retains these while needed for enabled features. Unlinking or opting out clears or invalidates the related derived state. Failed cleanup can leave pending retry work or inaccessible cached bytes until removal succeeds; system-managed updates may take time.
CloudKit Relay data
Disabling Relay or unlinking on the publishing device requests deletion of its active cloud record. Disabling a receiver clears that receiver's imported state. It does not delete my original vault or another device's independent source data. Cloud deletion requires service availability.
Apple Health records
Stopping synchronization does not automatically delete previously written Health entries. A minimal local ownership ledger may remain after unlinking to manage entries and prevent duplicate exports. I can change permissions or remove TishOS-created records in Apple Health. TishOS does not delete records owned by another source.
Watch journals and drafts
These local records and pending transfers are independent of vault linking. Disconnecting a vault does not erase them, an exported document, or a workout already saved to Apple Health. Removing a copy on one device should not be assumed to remove every paired copy.
Vault files, exports, and AI requests
They remain ordinary files until I or the responsible workflow removes them. Unlinking or uninstalling TishOS does not erase synchronized copies, file history, or backups. Those are managed through the vault and storage provider.

Uninstalling the app also does not guarantee removal of Keychain items, shared extension data, cloud records, or Apple Health entries. Where possible, I use the relevant disconnect controls first and manage independent records in the service that owns them.

8. Security and limitations

The app uses platform folder authorization, sandboxing, protected local storage where applicable, separate credential storage, and validation of supported routes and paired messages. Supported actions recheck the current vault and destination. These protections do not make an ordinary Markdown file, visible notification, or shared backup secret.

Access to my unlocked devices, vault, or authorized storage accounts can expose data held there. Encryption and retention for provider-held copies depend on the provider and my configuration. I do not claim absolute security or end-to-end encryption of all app data.

Local diagnostics are designed to record bounded counts, stages, and outcomes rather than full note contents, credentials, or settings dumps. They are not automatically sent to a developer analytics service. Operating-system and plugin logs can have different contents.

9. Website visits and correspondence

This public website has no analytics scripts, advertising, forms, cookies, or browser storage of its own. GitHub Pages hosts it and logs visitor IP addresses for security purposes under GitHub's privacy statement. Links to Apple, GitHub, or other sites lead to services with their own policies.

If someone opens a GitHub issue about this project, their GitHub identity, message, and any attachments are public. I use that information to respond or maintain the project. It can remain in the issue history; GitHub controls its own retained copies. Please do not submit private financial or health records, notes, passwords, or other credentials.

Apple separately processes installation, diagnostic, crash, and feedback information associated with TestFlight under its policies and applicable settings. This testing infrastructure does not change the fact that I am currently the app's only user.

10. Changes and contact

I will update this document when the app's data handling changes. If the project later gains other users or broader distribution, I will revisit its privacy disclosures and distribution requirements. This policy describes its current personal-use context and explicitly identified development plans.

For questions about the project, or correspondence provided to me, contact ZachTish through the website repository. Posting requires a GitHub account. Start with non-sensitive details because that channel is public. Requests about information independently held by Apple or GitHub need to go to the relevant provider.
