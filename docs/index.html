How it works

One workspace. Three distinct jobs.

TishOS is a system of cooperating tools. The native app, Obsidian plugins, and vault each have a different responsibility.

01

The vault holds your records

Notes, properties, links, and supported structured records live in your chosen Obsidian vault. A calendar, list, or summary is a way of looking at those records. Its presentation does not have to become another disconnected copy of your information.

02

TPS plugins provide the workflows

Inside Obsidian, compatible TPS plugins provide tools such as calendars, contextual editing, reminders, and domain-specific logging. Their settings define the supported record structure and behavior. Controller coordinates the automations and integrations it owns; each plugin retains its own responsibilities.

03

The native app connects to Apple features

The TishOS companion reads your authorized vault and prepares limited local views for widgets, notifications, Live Activities, and optional Siri or Spotlight access. Supported actions open the appropriate Obsidian note, view, or plugin workflow. The companion is not a full Markdown editor and does not run Obsidian plugins inside a widget.

The methodology

Keep the record useful beyond one screen.

The organizing idea is simple: information should have an understandable home, a stable identity, and relationships to the other things it concerns.

A note can be a unit of work

An atomic note represents one independently useful record: a task, an account, an exercise, or an event. Its properties make it filterable; its body keeps the explanation, context, and links close by. Supported workflows can also use an atomic line for a smaller record inside a note.

Separate things from what happens

An entity describes something that persists, such as an account or an exercise. A transaction describes an occurrence, such as a purchase or a workout entry. Linking occurrences to stable things makes history easier to review without overwriting the thing itself.

Use views to answer questions

The same records can support different questions: what is scheduled today, what is still open, or what contributed to a total? A saved Base view supplies supported filters, ordering, and presentation. The native companion materializes supported views for Apple surfaces; it does not reproduce every Obsidian formula or custom JavaScript view.

Make automation accountable

Use configured rules and properties to express intent. Preserve the distinction between a user's record and a derived display. Supported integrations use stable identifiers and guarded updates so refreshing a view need not mean creating the same record again.

A concrete example

An account and its transactions

An account note can hold the account's identity and context. Separate transaction records can describe individual purchases and link back to that account. A monthly spending view then summarizes those records, while each purchase remains inspectable on its own.

“Entity” and “transaction” describe the organizing approach. They are not a promise that every plugin already shares one universal schema or that those words must be your property names. Storage modes, supported types, and configurable keys still depend on the owning plugin.

From a record to an action

Bring the right context to the right place.

Plan from a calendar or list

Choose a supported named Base view for a widget. Its filters and ordering determine what appears. A tap returns to the relevant view; supported line actions can hand off to the plugin's editor while keeping the surrounding context.

Follow reminders and active work

Controller's Reminder Rules determine reminder eligibility and timing. The companion verifies the paired schedule and asks Apple's notification system to deliver it. Separately enabled Live Activities can display a selected calendar's active item or a supported running timer.

Review daily summaries

Macro summaries and other supported projections turn compatible vault records into a glanceable view. Optional Apple Health integration has separate controls for selected nutrition writes and daily activity reads. A summary widget is a view of the supported data, not an independent nutrition database.

Act through familiar entry points

Supported Shortcuts, App Intents, and widget commands provide quick entry points into an authorized workflow. A visible handoff to Obsidian lets the owning plugin perform its action. Availability depends on the installed app, plugins, permissions, and operating system.

Setup and device boundaries

Connect deliberately, one device at a time.

  1. Choose your vault

    Use the native folder picker to authorize the intended vault on each device. TishOS scans supported records and prepares local display data. That choice does not automatically grant access to other folders.

  2. Pair Controller where needed

    Approve the companion's connection through Obsidian for supported commands and signed reminder schedules. Vault access and Controller pairing are separate connections; one does not silently grant the other.

  3. Select your surfaces

    Choose each widget's view or note. Enable notifications, Live Activities, Health access, or search integration only where useful. Some features require an additional disclosure or Apple permission.

  4. Choose whether to use Relay

    Optional Relay shares supported, limited workspace views through your Apple account's private iCloud database. It can help another device display current context. It is separate from the service syncing your vault files and does not replace attachment sync or make the entire vault available offline.

Background updates have real limits

Apple controls background execution, notification delivery, Live Activity updates, and widget refreshes. Vault synchronization and file availability also take time. A feature can be configured correctly and still wait for data or an opportunity to run. TishOS reports connection and refresh state; it does not promise continuous iPhone execution or instant updates everywhere.

Planned · Apple approval required

Apple Wallet as another source for your records.

We are pursuing FinanceKit approval for a read-only connection to supported Apple Card, Savings, and Apple Cash accounts. This feature is not implemented or available yet.

The intended flow is an explicitly authorized iPhone reading selected accounts, balances, and transactions, then delivering them to the user's paired Controller device through an encrypted vault relay. TPS Finances would maintain account and transaction notes for budgeting, spending review, and net-worth tracking. The resulting notes would follow the user's normal vault storage and sync settings.

This keeps the financial record inspectable and connected to the rest of the workspace. It does not mean the companion already has a bank connection, and it is not a plan to initiate payments. Approval, implementation, consent and deletion controls, and physical-device validation must precede availability.

Current availability

The native companion is in internal TestFlight testing for iPhone, iPad, and Mac. It is not publicly available in the App Store. The current platform baseline is iOS/iPadOS 18 and macOS 26; individual features require newer versions or compatible hardware.

Some internal builds also include Watch workout journals and quick-capture drafts. Paired delivery and physical-device validation remain in testing. Draft export is separate from automatically creating a TPS food, workout, or finance record.

Features, data compatibility, and platform coverage vary by build. The companion's public information site does not make internal builds available, change permissions, or establish Apple approval for future integrations.
