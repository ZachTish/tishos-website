TishOS

TishOS is my personal experiment in bringing the data from my life into Markdown. I built it for myself, and I am currently its only user.

I use Obsidian as the place where I keep and work with those files. I am building plugins and a small native Apple companion app to connect the parts that would otherwise require separate tools or manual copying.

This website documents the project and its data handling for anyone reviewing it, including Apple. The app is in personal development and internal TestFlight testing. There is no public app download or sign-up here.

Why Markdown

I want my notes, tasks, calendar events, food and workout logs, and financial records to live in a format I can read, edit, link, and keep using as my tools change. Markdown lets a record contain both structured properties and the ordinary explanation that belongs with it.

For example, an account can have its own note, while individual purchases are separate records linked to it. An exercise can have a note, while a workout records what I did on a particular day. I think of the persistent things as entities and the things that happen as transactions.

That is an organizing idea I am working toward, not a claim that every plugin already uses the same schema. Some supported records live in their own notes, which I call atomic notes; others are structured lines inside a note, or atomic lines. Property names, record types, and storage modes depend on the plugin and my configuration.

A calendar, list, or summary then becomes a view of the records. I want to be able to go from a total or a scheduled item back to the note that explains it.

How the pieces work

  • The Obsidian vault contains my Markdown files, properties, links, and attachments.
  • The TPS plugins provide the editing, calendar, logging, and automation features inside Obsidian. Controller handles the reminders and external integrations it owns. Other plugins handle their own records and workflows.
  • The native TishOS app connects the authorized vault to Apple features such as widgets, notifications, Live Activities, Shortcuts, and optional Siri or Spotlight access. It prepares limited local views and opens the relevant Obsidian workflow when needed. It does not run Obsidian plugins inside a widget or replace Obsidian as my editor.

I authorize the vault on each device. Controller pairing is a separate step for supported commands and signed reminder schedules. Apple Health, search indexing, notifications, and other optional features have their own controls and permissions.

Most processing is local. Optional cross-device Relay uses my Apple account's private CloudKit database for limited display data. My vault's file-sync service is separate and can copy files between devices. The system is therefore not strictly offline or confined to one device.

Current state

I am developing and testing the companion on my Apple devices through internal TestFlight. It has iPhone, iPad, and Mac targets, with a baseline of iOS/iPadOS 18 and macOS 26. Individual features need newer systems or compatible hardware.

The work includes optional nutrition export and daily activity reads through Apple Health, as well as experimental Watch workout journals and capture drafts. A draft or exported document is not automatically an imported food, workout, or financial transaction.

Parts of the system are unfinished. Apple controls background execution and widget updates, and file synchronization can introduce delays. I am not claiming that every integration is complete, that refreshes are immediate, or that the internal builds are approved for public distribution.

Why I am requesting FinanceKit access

I use Apple Card and Apple Savings and would like to bring their balances and transactions into the same personal Markdown system. FinanceKit would let the native app read my authorized Apple Wallet information, rather than requiring me to copy it manually.

The proposed connection would read supported account details, balances, and transactions on my iPhone. An encrypted relay through my vault's sync provider would deliver that information to my paired Controller device, where TPS Finances would maintain account and transaction notes. I would use those records for my own budgeting, spending review, and net-worth tracking. Apple Cash could use the same connection if I authorize it.

This FinanceKit connection is not implemented or approved yet. I am requesting access for development and testing with my Individual Apple Developer membership. No payment initiation is planned. The native app's bundle ID is com.zachtisherman.tishos.

The privacy policy separates this proposed data flow from the features that already exist.
